# Wine comparisons, taste profiles and Cerebras

**Planning refinement, September 5, 2026.** Companion to the reviewed AI sommelier plan. No implementation, dependencies, inference spending or pilot-scope change. The prior signed document remains intact; this refinement makes AI-3's content-similarity requirement explicit and distinguishes it from AI-6's later behavioral item similarity.

**Decision:** preserve the architecture and strengthen its comparison contract. Support multiple explicit relationships between wines. Sensory resemblance is one view; producer identity, related producers, origin and regional vintage conditions are others. Personal enjoyment and commercial eligibility remain separate. A purchase/rating score alone cannot describe these relationships.

## What similar means in the product

| Comparison mode | Meaning and computation | What the interface must explain |
|---|---|---|
| Similar taste profile | Compare evidence-backed taste, aroma and mouthfeel dimensions, then apply the user's requested variation and constraints | Shared traits, important differences, evidence coverage and vintage scope |
| Same producer | Exact canonical producer relationship with aliases resolved; distinguish producer, brand, importer and parent company roles | Same named producer does not imply the same grapes, vintage, region or style |
| Similar producers | Compare supported portfolio styles, grape/region footprint and documented winemaking practices; return facet-specific reasons | Similar in which respects, and which wines/claims support it; no inferred shared ownership or blanket quality score |
| Same region | Explicit grape-origin/appellation relationships at the requested geographic level | Napa versus a named sub-appellation; multiple-origin blends remain explicit; winery address is not grape origin |
| Regional vintage comparison | Compare harvest-year conditions within a region or explicitly compare matched region/year contexts | Rainfall timing, temperature/heat, radiation or measured sunshine, source coverage, and any attributed vintage assessment |
| Personal match | Rank eligible choices using current intent, taste anchors, ratings and separately interpreted repeat purchases | Why this person may enjoy it, with actual evidence; a different quantity from wine similarity |

Use a comparison control with these clear names rather than one opaque similarity percentage. A request can combine them: same producer, a different vintage, drier profile and a stated budget. Contradictory combinations prompt a short clarification rather than silently changing the request. The user can browse producer/region relationships without a personal profile. Knowledge-page navigation and contextual explanations should remain available when sensory coverage is insufficient.

## Producer, region and vintage evidence

Add source-attributed producer roles and wine-origin relationships to the shared catalog. Retain claim validity/version and grape-origin share only when known. Related-producer results need evidence from a declared eligible portion of the portfolio, minimum coverage and disclosure of what is missing; one atypical bottle must not silently represent an entire producer. Use relational links and versioned comparison features; this does not require a graph database.

A regional vintage record is keyed by geographic scope, harvest year and metric-definition version. Store source geometry/stations or grid resolution, observation/growing-season windows, units, completeness/quality flags, aggregation method and a defined historical reference period for anomalies. Rainfall total alone hides timing; seasonal and ripening-period conditions require explicitly defined windows. Respect hemispheres, local growing cycles and known variety/harvest differences. Solar radiation and recorded sunshine duration are different measurements and must retain their names and units.

Observed weather, derived climate summaries, attributed producer/critic vintage commentary and inferred effects on wine are separate evidence types. Regional weather is contextual evidence, not a measured bottle trait or a direct wine-quality verdict. Compare only commensurate metrics; label missing weather rather than inventing it. Even within an appellation, vineyard water supply, soil, irrigation, canopy, harvest decisions, grape blend and winemaking can differ. Keep aged-bottle condition separate from harvest-year weather.

For USA-first data exploration, [NOAA GHCN-Daily](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/global-historical-climatology-network-daily) supplies station-based daily temperature and precipitation observations. [NASA POWER's daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) provides solar and meteorological data. These are candidate inputs, not validated vineyard-level feeds. Before ingestion, verify the chosen variables, spatial/temporal coverage, units, revision/quality semantics and reuse terms. No weather ingestion or model has been built in this research.

The [2018 soil/terroir review](https://doi.org/10.20870/oeno-one.2018.52.2.2208) describes interacting water, soil and climate influences, including nonlinear responses to water deficit. A [2024 Corbières study](https://doi.org/10.20870/oeno-one.2024.58.3.7921) compared blends across five sub-regions and two vintages using chemistry and descriptive sensory profiles; blend composition and winemaking mattered, and the authors cautioned that the direct terroir effect needed more controlled study. These support contextual, multi-factor comparisons. They do not justify rules such as more sunlight always means a better wine, or wetter means more acidic.


## Cerebras's role

The current [Cerebras documentation index](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/llms.txt) lists chat/completion, tool calling and structured-output capabilities. The [public model catalog](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/models/overview) lists generative models. No hosted embedding endpoint was verified in the inspected documentation. This does not establish that dedicated/custom arrangements can never support one.

Cerebras's own [Milvus RAG example](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/integrations/milvus) uses Cerebras for chat completions and leaves embedding generation to a separate provider or local model. Its example includes random placeholder vectors, which must not be copied into a real retrieval system. Integration with a vector database does not mean Cerebras supplies its embeddings.

Therefore, Cerebras is a candidate for request parsing, source-backed descriptor extraction and conversational explanations. Trial data classes are explicit: synthetic requests and independently sourced, rights-approved public evidence may be considered under the current research authorization; real user text, private notes and profile-derived explanations require the approved inference boundary or an explicit amendment before any inference call. This run spends no Cerebras credits. Keep embedding-model selection separate: the existing Qwen3/E5/BGE shortlist remains an evaluation shortlist. An extraction model may propose a descriptor only from cited evidence and must preserve its subjective/source status; it cannot invent missing sensory measurements. Credits do not settle runtime quality, credit expiry, model access, data retention or hosting. Before evaluation, verify actual organization/model access through an authenticated model-list or capability check; the public API probe in this run returned HTTP 403. ## Wine profiles and similarity

The proposed profile separates these dimensions. Field names and scales still need wine-reviewer calibration; this is a product schema informed by sensory research, not a universally validated wine taxonomy.

| Profile dimension | Representation |
|---|---|
| Perceived sweetness | Dry through sweet, with the original descriptor and tasting context; distinct from residual sugar measurements |
| Perceived acidity | Sourness/tartness and supported intensity; normalize overlapping language without counting tart and acidic twice |
| Astringency and bitterness | Separate mouth-drying/puckering sensation from bitter taste; retain tannin descriptions without automatically converting chemical tannin content into either intensity |
| Body and mouthfeel | Supported body/texture descriptors; alcohol warmth and effervescence separately when applicable; no automatic ABV-to-body conversion |
| Aroma and flavor | Supported fruit, floral, herbal, spice, earth and oak-related descriptors, intensity and persistence where reported; distinguish an oak-like impression from a documented barrel-aging fact |
| Measured composition | Residual sugar, pH, titratable acidity and ABV when available, preserving units, method, sample/vintage and provenance; no automatic conversion to subjective taste scores |

An ambiguous user phrase such as “too dry” may mean insufficient sweetness or a mouth-drying sensation. Ask a short contextual clarification when that distinction changes the recommendation. Preserve the user's words and confirmed meaning.

### What the sensory literature supports

| Primary study | Bounded finding | Implication for this plan |
|---|---|---|
| [Torri et al., 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950329313000104): aroma mapping and descriptive analysis of 11 Italian reds | Expert/consumer groupings and liking did not describe the same relationships; descriptive and mapping spaces differed | Evaluate sensory resemblance separately from enjoyment and quality judgments |
| [Liu et al., 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950329315002025): controlled aroma changes in a Pinot Blanc base | Training improved projective mapping; different methods captured qualitative versus intensity differences differently | Define attribute scales, familiarization and repeatability checks; include blind duplicates in reviewer work |
| [Zamora et al., 2006](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1745-459X.2006.00085.x): nine trained assessors, acid/sugar mixtures in different media | Sweetness and sourness interacted, with responses differing by medium | Keep perceived taste and composition separate; this supplies no universal chemistry-to-taste conversion |
| [Laguna et al., 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/8/6/190): six model wines, eight trained assessors and nine experts | Body, astringency, bitterness and alcohol sensation were scored separately; instrumental viscosity did not significantly correlate with perceived body in this experiment | Preserve distinct sensory dimensions and assessor context instead of using one chemical or physical proxy |

These are sensory experiments, not validations of a production recommendation algorithm. The first three were checked through publisher abstracts/previews; the fourth through full text. Their small or constrained wine sets limit generalization; the fourth used one saliva donor for instrumental tests. The proposed application fields and retrieval approach are our design inferences. Wine-specific accuracy still requires the independent evaluation described below.

Build a versioned sensory representation for each resolved wine edition: producer/cuvée plus vintage state, linked to package identities. Preserve vintage-specific evidence; an unknown-vintage record is not evidence for a particular vintage. Cuvée-level evidence may contribute only as an explicitly generic, lower-confidence layer when the source claim actually describes the cuvée generally; show that scope and let vintage-specific evidence take precedence. A tasting of an unknown vintage is not automatically general cuvée evidence. Non-vintage is a distinct product state, not the unknown-year fallback. Coverage and explanations must distinguish these layers. The original “last nine” counting grain remains unchanged. Package and bottle-condition differences remain visible context, not silently assumed sensory equivalence.

Represent body, perceived acidity, tannin, sweetness, oak, fruit character and other supported descriptors with their source, date, scale, confidence and disagreement. Record value origin (author-stated, normalized from a stated descriptor, or extractor-inferred), source span, normalization/schema revision, extractor model/version and prompt revision. Validate extraction against reviewer-labeled samples before inferred values enter shared profiles; an extractor change creates a new revision and invalidates dependents. Preserve inferred status in comparisons and never attribute inferred numeric intensity to the original author. Measured chemistry and subjective tasting impressions remain separate. Grape, region and winemaking facts can help retrieve candidates; shared Napa/Cabernet labels alone do not establish sensory equivalence. Price, prestige, sponsorship, retailer and current stock are excluded from the sensory-similarity calculation. They can constrain availability, purchase mode or budget separately.

Missing is unknown, never zero. A sparsely described wine must not appear especially similar merely because few dimensions are comparable. The first implementation needs a minimum evidence-coverage rule, scale normalization, explicit categorical handling and abstention. Freeze their exact definitions with the schema owner and wine reviewers before implementation and evaluate them against labeled examples; this document does not declare a best metric or universal threshold.

Use structured sensory comparison as the initial baseline. Evaluate text embeddings of evidence-backed tasting descriptions as a separate candidate source, then evaluate a combined method. Exclude brand, price and promotional prose from the sensory text representation. A generic text embedding's cosine similarity is not a calibrated taste similarity or probability of enjoyment. Embedding trials remain subject to corpus, hosting and model-configuration gates. The per-source rights manifest separately determines ingestion, extraction/derived-vector storage, shared-index versus private-overlay use, retention and user-facing display permissions. Permission to cite a source does not imply the other permissions. Resolve these before the embedding trial and before freezing comparison cards.

Offer **Similar in taste** on a wine page, including shared characteristics, meaningful differences and evidence quality. Style groups are overlapping navigation aids, not one fixed class per wine and not a prerequisite for retrieval. Start with nearest neighbors and supported descriptive tags; add clustering only if users find it useful. A user can explore similar wines from other regions or ask for a deliberate variation such as less oak. Explicit style changes alter the task; they should not be portrayed as the closest sensory match.

## Personal taste and repeated purchases

Preserve several liked/disliked wine anchors and request contexts rather than averaging all tastes into one profile vector. A person may enjoy both dry Riesling and full-bodied Cabernet. For a Napa Cabernet request, use the relevant evidence, current constraints and negative preferences, and cite the exact supporting wines.

Store every real acquisition and rating with its existing provenance. Repeated personal purchases are a separate behavioral signal: distinguish separate purchase occasions from bottles within one case, replayed imports, gifts, restaurant procurement and returns. Repeated voluntary personal purchases can increase the evidence of interest, but never silently become an explicit positive rating. A low personal rating is not overridden merely by purchase volume. Proposed recency/strength rules must be calibrated; no arbitrary event-point total is treated as truth.

The nine-distinct-wine explanation and repeat frequency are separate features. Buying one edition repeatedly still contributes one edition to “last nine wines”; its eligible repeat occasions may be disclosed separately. Do not invent a rating or make an explanation imply that nine purchases had nine known prices or nine positive experiences.

Later, AI-6 may learn behavioral item-to-item relationships from appropriately consented, exposure-aware history. Such relationships can mean co-interest or substitution, not necessarily similar taste. Keep them labeled and separate from sensory neighbors. The existing retention, deletion, historical-eligibility, purpose and no-sponsored-ranking rules continue to apply.

## Storage and retrieval contract

Conceptual records, not applied tables:

| Record | Purpose and required boundary |
|---|---|
| Wine sensory profile | Edition, trait values, source/scale/confidence, evidence coverage and revision. A shared profile uses rights-approved public evidence only. Private notes form scoped overlays. |
| Comparison configuration | Mode, feature definitions, distance/matching method, missing-value/coverage rules, source scope and version; embedding configuration separately versioned if used. |
| Producer/origin links | Canonical producer roles, wine grape-origin/appellation links and claim validity; a producer address cannot substitute for grape origin. |
| Regional vintage context | Region/harvest-year, metric windows/units, weather-source geometry, coverage, aggregation/reference period and provenance; climate summaries separate from attributed vintage commentary. |
| Wine neighbors | Optional rebuildable top-k cache: source edition, neighbor, configuration, component comparisons, evidence and revisions. Public and private caches never mix. No full pairwise matrix or graph database required. |
| Personal anchors and signals | Existing profile-owned ratings, acquisitions, repeat occasions and explicit preferences. Multiple taste contexts remain possible; private behavior never enters shared sensory profiles automatically. |
| Recommendation decision | Authorized eligible candidate set/snapshot, source modes, profile/source revisions, evidence contributions and exposure/feedback references, as required by the original plan. |

At request time: resolve wine/intent and authorized scope; apply mandatory constraints; route each selected comparison mode to exact producer/origin joins, regional-vintage context or sensory/portfolio comparison as appropriate; personalize only where requested using relevant anchors; revalidate eligibility and privacy; render supported comparisons. Keep exact stock/location on live SQL. Cached global top-k sensory neighbors are not a complete eligible search: when budget/availability filters remove them, fall back to a constrained-set exact scan and rank of structured sensory profiles, or report insufficient evidence. Pre-estimation sizing must measure eligible-set size and p95 latency against the largest proposed launch slice; there is no assumed catalog size at which scanning is guaranteed fast. Use bounded scans only with visible incompleteness; add indexing or narrower explicit filters if the latency budget fails, rather than silently returning a cache-only result. Never relax a hard constraint silently.

Corrections, withdrawal and profile erasure invalidate dependent profiles, vectors, neighbor caches and decision evidence under the existing revision/retention protocol. Model jobs cannot publish private overlays into shared indexes. A typed server tool owns retrieval; an LLM supplies no SQL or authorization.
